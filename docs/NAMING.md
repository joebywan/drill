# Why Wingbeat

The app was called Drill until November 2026. This is how it became Wingbeat, so the reasons don't
get lost.

## The problem with Drill

"Drill" came from drilling by repetition, but it sounds industrial and gritty, like a power tool, and
the app had moved from drilling to growing. Its real subject is a daily habit: a little learning,
self-awareness or improvement each day, across two halves (Learn and Practice) that pull a name in
opposite directions.

## How the name was chosen

Every round started with a clash check (app stores, brands, slang, katakana), marking same-niche
clashes apart from other-field ones. Councils of 15 personas (professionals and users of different
ages, backgrounds and motivation) then reviewed the survivors.

1. **Garden names** (Sprout, Tend, Grove, Bloom, Sunpatch…). Most were taken in the niche, often by
   near-identical apps. Sunpatch survived by elimination and didn't land.
2. **A brief on the theme problem.** The council's answer: the garden is one reward, not the brand;
   Lumi leads; name the behaviour, not the reward; one name over plain Learn and Practise.
3. **Lumicove** won 15 of 15 but didn't land with the owner.
4. **The owner's two ideas:** momentum (hard to start, easier as it builds) and the butterfly effect
   (each small action changes your future). Most words for both were taken. **Wingbeat** was clean:
   one wingbeat is the butterfly effect's image, take-off is the hardest part of flight, and a beat
   is a rhythm.
5. **Wingbeat's stress test:** 15 of 15 backed it, each with fixes (below).
6. **Trade marks:** clear in Australia; nothing in our classes in the US, UK or Japan. The EU has
   "wingbeat-house" (EUTM 019357623, registered September 2026), covering educational software and
   mobile apps. Words from other languages were checked as alternatives (Habataki, Lumipallo, and the
   blend Habitaki), and a final council kept Wingbeat 14 to 1.

## Decisions

- Name **Wingbeat**, always one word. Tagline **"Small beats go a long way."**
- The word "streak" stays.
- Lumi stays the guide and the face of the icon, with a butterfly on her head.
- The butterflies carry the wings, never Lumi or a bird. A butterfly-wing costume with arm straps is
  fine, because it's plainly dress-up.
- "Beat" only ever means a rhythm or a wing, never winning a score.
- The store listing leads with Learn, so the name doesn't read as a rhythm game.
- The EU mark: get a trade mark lawyer's opinion before the Play launch, launch outside the EU
  first, and keep a fallback ready ("Wingbeat Learn", or Habataki for the EU only).

## Ideas that came with the name, not built yet

- Your own butterfly, whose wings fill in with colour as you practise, flying, basking or resting
  to show today.
- A butterfly for every course, growing from egg to wings as the course goes, each a real Australian
  species.
- Skins for Lumi, and a field guide with a line or two of true facts per species.
